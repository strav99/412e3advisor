Before you ask

What does the feasibility analysis cost?

Nothing. We model the deductible contribution and projected accumulation from your age, income, and entity structure, with a side-by-side comparison to traditional defined-benefit and cash balance designs. You see real numbers before any commitment.

Is a 412(e)(3) legitimate?

Yes — 412(e)(3) is the current code section for what was formerly 412(i), an IRS-qualified defined-benefit plan. A properly designed, conservatively funded plan with clean disclosures is fully sanctioned. That conservative design is exactly the work we do.

How much can I actually deduct?

Far more than the plans most owners already know. A SEP-IRA or solo 401(k) caps out around $70,000 a year; a 412(e)(3) commonly deducts $100,000 to $400,000+. The exact figure is set by the IRS maximum-benefit limit for defined-benefit plans and by your income, and comes out of the analysis.
